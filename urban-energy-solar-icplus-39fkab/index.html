The Flexibility You Already Use

You already run two ways of handling the fee. On an install you add it to the customer's invoice. On a service call or a deposit you absorb it. None of that has to change, and the same account does both without you deciding in advance.

  • Keep absorbing it where you absorb it today. Service calls and deposits stay exactly as they are, just at a lower cost to you.
  • Keep adding it where you add it today. On installs you add the fee the same way, and because your own cost comes down, the line the customer sees on a big job comes down with it.
  • If the owner ever wants the card cost off the business entirely, there is a program that moves it to the card-paying customer across the board, with cash, check and bank transfer customers paying the listed price. Same account, same workflow, no new application. Worth a conversation once you have a few real months in front of you.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • You stop paying a flat rate on cards that are not flat. Square charges 3.3% to 3.5% whether the customer pays with a basic debit card or a premium rewards card. Here you pay what the card actually costs plus a flat 0.50% and 8 cents, so the cheap cards finally cost you less.
  • A card payment costs you about 20% less. About 2.6% to 2.9% all-in against the 3.3% to 3.5% you run today. Estimated, because the blend moves with the cards your customers use.
  • When you add the fee on an install, the customer's number gets smaller too. You are adding 3.5% today. On this pricing you are covering closer to 2.7%, so the line on a big invoice comes down with your cost. That is an easier conversation on a $20,000 job.
  • Bank transfers are capped at $10. On a deposit, a percentage of the whole amount is the most expensive way to get paid. A bank transfer here is 0.50% and it stops at ten dollars, whatever the deposit is.
  • No contract, no setup fee, no cancellation fee. And American Express prices the same as Visa and Mastercard, on one deposit, with no separate AMEX bill.
