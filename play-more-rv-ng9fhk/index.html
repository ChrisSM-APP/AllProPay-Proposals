Where We Are

You have been on a flat rate for three and a half years and you told me you shop rates on everything. So this is not a pitch. You take cards three ways: in person on the terminal, keyed in over the phone for down payments, and online. Those three are priced very differently, and that turns out to be the whole story.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • Nothing about how you ring up a sale changes. Terminal on the desk, phone orders keyed in the same way, online the same way.
  • You keep absorbing the fee. You said you are not passing it to the customer, so nothing in here asks you to.

  • You get the real number before you decide, not a sales estimate. If it is not worth moving, we will say so.

Why This Is the Right Move
  • You are paying 3.5 percent to punch in a card over the phone. That is the most expensive way to take a card on a flat rate, and it is exactly how you take down-payments. On interchange plus, that same keyed card lands closer to 2.8 percent.
  • Your fixed cost is $15 a month. No contract, no minimum, no cancellation fee. On a slow month the account just sits there.
  • You can see the split. Interchange on one line, our flat 0.6 percent on another. You told me you shop rates. This is the pricing model that lets you actually do it.
  • The savings here are real but small. Estimated $0 to $30 a month at your volume, depending on your card mix. We are not going to dress that up into a number it is not.
  • Nothing changes about how you run the lot. Same terminal, same phone orders, same online. We do the switch-over work.

Here is the math on the $8,000 month you described. On a flat rate, an in-person card runs you 2.6 percent, an online sale 2.9 percent, and a card you key in over the phone 3.5 percent. Blended, that is roughly $240 a month, which lines up with the $250 we ballparked on the phone. On interchange plus 0.6 percent and 5 cents, that same $8,000 lands somewhere around $210 to $240 in processing plus the $15 platform fee. Call it $225 to $255 all-in against about $240 today. So depending on your mix, you keep roughly $0 to $30 a month, and on a $5,000 month it is smaller still. That is the honest range. The one number that decides where you land is how much of your volume is keyed in over the phone, and that is a two minute look in your Square dashboard. These are estimates based on the volume and mix you shared, not a guarantee.
