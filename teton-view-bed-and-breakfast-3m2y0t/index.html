Where We Are

Carol, here is what I took away from our call, in plain terms.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • You have been on the same processing setup for years, and when I asked who it actually runs through, the honest answer was that you would have to look it up. That is not a knock on you. It means nobody has put your own numbers in front of you in a very long time.
  • Bookings run through ThinkReservations on the Authorize.net gateway, with a third-party processor sitting behind it setting what you pay.
  • You collect roughly $160,000 to $180,000 a year across a nine-month season, January through September, and you do not process October through December.

What You Get
Nothing about your bookings changes
ThinkReservations stays embedded on your site and Authorize.net stays the gateway. Guests book tomorrow exactly the way they booked yesterday. There is no new system to learn, no reservation history to move, and no downtime in the middle of your season. What changes is the cost underneath, not the way you run.
One markup, and it is written down
You pay interchange, the true cost set by Visa and Mastercard, plus a flat 0.50 percent and 10 cents a transaction. That is the entire markup, and it lives in your agreement as a number rather than as something somebody said on a phone call. Interchange itself moves when the card brands move it, twice a year, and every processor passes that through. What we do not do is quietly widen our own piece while you are not looking.
A real person and a nine-month account
You get my direct line rather than a support queue, and the account is set up around a January to September season instead of billing you like a business that never closes. No contract, no cancellation fee, and the Authorize.net gateway goes to you at our cost rather than marked up.

Built Around Your Season

You collect January through September and go quiet October through December. Most processors bill an account the same twelve months a year whether it is working or not, so three of those months you are paying to keep a door open that is already closed. Your account gets built around the nine months you actually run.

  • Set up for a nine-month season. January through September is how the account is configured, not an exception we make for you.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee. Nothing to unwind if you change your mind, in season or out.
  • Switch-over timed around you. Nothing moves in the middle of your busy stretch. Your booking page never goes down.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • One markup, in writing. Interchange plus a flat 0.50 percent and 10 cents a transaction. That is everything that sits on top of the true cost of the card, and it is a number in the agreement rather than a rate somebody described to you.
  • Your booking flow does not move. ThinkReservations stays, Authorize.net stays. No new system, no data migration, nothing to relearn in the middle of your season.
  • The gateway goes to you at our cost. Whatever the third-party processor is currently adding on top of your Authorize.net account comes off.
  • Built for nine months, not twelve. Your season runs January through September, and the account is configured around that rather than billing you year-round for months you do not process.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee. Thirty years in, you have heard enough promises. If we stop earning it, you leave.

On roughly $160,000 to $180,000 a year, every tenth of a percent is about $170, which is exactly why I would rather show you the real gap than estimate at you. Your volume is card-not-present through a booking engine, and card-not-present carries higher interchange than a card tapped at a front desk, so an honest all-in estimate for an account built like yours lands somewhere around 2.4 to 2.9 percent once the true cost of the cards your guests actually use is included. Whether that beats what you have today depends entirely on what the third-party processor behind your gateway is charging you, and right now neither of us knows that number. One statement from a busy month settles it. These are estimates rather than a guarantee, and I would rather be exact than optimistic.
