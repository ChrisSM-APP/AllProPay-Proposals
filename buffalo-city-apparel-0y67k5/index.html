What We Found

1. Invoiced transactions are priced higher than tap transactions

When a customer pays by link, the card is treated as keyed / card-not-present by the networks. That is a less secure category than tapping a physical reader in front of you, so the interchange cost is meaningfully higher. This is universal - Halcyon, AllProPay, or anyone else.

Because 95% of Buffalo City's volume is on the link side, the entire effective rate is being dragged up by that mix. There are two ways to fix it: move people to tap when possible, or reprice the link side directly.

2. The full processing cost is hidden inside your product prices

You mentioned you absorb the card cost rather than passing it through, but it is "built into pricing." That works, but it does two things you may not want: it commits margin to processing every month whether the customer pays by card or check, and it hides the line item so it never gets pressure-tested.

A 3% drag on $20,000/month is $7,200/year in margin that is currently invisible. Worth deciding whether you want to keep absorbing it (at a cleaner rate) or shift it back to the card-paying customer entirely.

3. Wholesale B2B mix is a real lever

Business cards (corporate, purchasing, fleet) have their own interchange categories, and B2B-heavy accounts often qualify for Level 2 / Level 3 data processing, which lowers the rate per swipe when invoice line-item data is passed with the transaction. Halcyon's virtual terminal may or may not be enabling that today. The statement will tell us.

Two Paths Forward

Both paths use AllProPay's virtual terminal and pay-by-link tools (functionally the same workflow Jordan uses today). The difference is who carries the card cost.

PATH A

Lower your rate, you keep absorbing the cost

Same setup, lower margin drag. Customers see no change.
  • Move to a clean interchange-plus structure - you pay actual network cost plus a small fixed markup, instead of bundled or tiered pricing.
  • For your B2B mix, we look at Level 2 / Level 3 data eligibility on the virtual terminal so corporate-card invoices drop into lower interchange categories.
  • Card cost stays absorbed in your pricing - exactly how you run today - but the underlying cost is lower, which means more of the margin you have already priced in stays with you.
  • Customer experience does not change. You still send a link, they still click and pay.
PATH B

Edge - automated 100% pass-through

Card cost moves off your P&L and onto the card-paying customer.
  • You enter the invoice amount at sticker price. Edge automatically adds the card surcharge at checkout to cover the processing cost.
  • Cash, check, and ACH payments are not surcharged - only card payments are. Compliant signage and customer-facing disclosure are handled.
  • Effectively removes the processing line from your P&L. The card-paying customer covers it, the network rules are followed, and you net the full invoice amount.
  • Same virtual-terminal workflow - sending a link, customer pays - just with the surcharge math automated on the back end.
Either path is fine. Path A is the lower-friction choice if you want zero customer-facing change. Path B is the bigger margin move if you are open to letting the surcharge show up on card payments. We will recommend the better fit once the statement is in.
