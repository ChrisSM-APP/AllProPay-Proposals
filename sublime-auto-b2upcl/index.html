Where We Are

Jude, you are already doing most of the hard part. You take cards at the counter and keyed over the phone, you add your own adjustment so the card cost does not come out of your margin, and you collect the in-house payments yourself every month. None of that has to change. What I want to fix is the piece stacked on top of the card cost, and the part where you key those monthly payments by hand.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • Keep taking cards both ways: the countertop machine for people who come to the lot, and keyed payments for the customers who pay over the phone.
  • Keep charging your customers exactly what you charge them today. Nothing your buyer sees has to change.
  • Put the monthly in-house payments on card on file so they run on a schedule instead of you keying them one at a time.
  • Get a real number on what you are actually paying, card type by card type, instead of "about two point something."

If You Would Rather Not Do the Math Yourself

You already pass the card cost to your customer, so you know how this works. If you would rather stop doing that math yourself, the same account runs our Edge program instead. The adjustment is calculated and disclosed automatically at the point of sale, and your cost on card sales is zero. Be clear-eyed about it though: on Edge you also stop keeping the difference you keep today, so it is a trade rather than an upgrade. It is worth doing only if the automation is worth more to you than that difference. Same application, same equipment, and you can move either direction later.

  • Start on the AllProPay Rate program and keep charging your customers exactly what you charge them today.
  • Move to Edge later on the same account if you would rather the machine handle the adjustment and the disclosure for you.
  • Run the monthly car notes over ACH if you want them cheaper still. A $500 payment clears for about $3 instead of card rates.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • You pay interchange plus a flat 0.50 to 0.70 percent. That is the whole markup, and it is the same on a debit card, a rewards card, and American Express.
  • Every point off your cost stays in your pocket. You already charge your customers an adjustment and you are not lowering it, so a lower cost is not a discount you pass on. It is margin.
  • The monthly in-house payments stop being a manual job. Card on file, charged on the date you set, with a decline notice when a card expires so nothing quietly stops paying.
  • You get me, not an 800 number. My cell and my email are on this page. When something needs fixing you call me and I handle it with the processor.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee. If we stop earning it, you leave.

Here is the math on a $12,500 month, the middle of the range you gave me. If Clover is adding about 1.2 percent over the true cost of the card, that is roughly $150 a month in markup. On the $6,000 down payment you mentioned, the markup alone drops from about $72 to about $30 to $42. These are estimates built on what you told me on the call, not a guarantee. One recent statement makes every one of these numbers exact, and if it turns out Clover is already cheaper than what I can do, I will tell you to stay where you are.
