Where We Are

You are already doing the thing I would recommend. You just are not doing it everywhere. On the call-ins you take through the Chase app, the card fee goes to the customer. On the jobs you invoice through QuickBooks, you eat it. Same business, same customers, two different answers, and the expensive answer is the one running on your biggest invoices. Everything below is about putting the whole business on the side you already picked, on one account, with your QuickBooks still in the middle of it.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • Your QuickBooks invoices are the expensive half. QuickBooks charges a flat 2.99% on card payments, and on the invoice side that comes out of your pocket. It is the same 2.99% whether the job is $400 or $9,000, and it does not go down as your volume goes up.
  • You already pass the fee on the Chase side. So this is not a new idea being sold to you. Your call-in customers already pay the card cost. Your invoice customers do not, and those are the bigger tickets.
  • Taking a check instead is not free either. QuickBooks bills about 1% on bank transfer and check payments through an invoice, capped at $20 a payment. Steering a customer off a card saves you most of the fee, but it costs you the wait.
  • Waiting is the real cost on a roof. You buy material before you get paid. Every invoice you push to a check to dodge a 2.99% fee is a week or two of your own money sitting in someone else's mailbox.
  • Two systems, one business. QuickBooks on one side, a Chase app on the other, and nothing talks to anything. One account with your QuickBooks synced to it fixes the reconciliation as a side effect.
What You Get
Zero-cost card processing
On Edge the card cost moves to the card-paying customer and your cost on card sales is $0. Cash, check, and bank transfer customers pay the invoice as written. That is the same arrangement you already run on your Chase call-ins, applied to the whole business instead of half of it.
Your QuickBooks stays exactly where it is
You keep invoicing out of QuickBooks the way you do now. Payments sync straight back against the invoice, so nothing gets marked paid by hand and nothing gets reconciled twice. The call-ins you take on the Chase app move onto the same account, and you tap the card on your phone from the roof or the truck. One deposit, one place to look, one person to call.
Backed and flexible
Direct underwriting, next-day funding, no contract and no cancellation fee. If you ever want to absorb the fee yourself instead, we move you to flat interchange-plus on the same account.

The Flexibility You Asked For

Two things I want to be straight with you about, because you should hear them from me now rather than find them out later.

  • The other program does not actually help you, and I am not going to pretend it does. Our other option, the AllProPay Rate, has you keep absorbing the cost but pay the true wholesale rate set by Visa and Mastercard plus a small flat markup. It is a real win for a lot of businesses. Yours is not one of them. Roofing invoices are large, they get paid by credit card rather than debit because nobody's debit card clears a $9,000 job, and the wholesale cost on that kind of card is already close to 2.4% before anyone adds a markup. By the time you add ours, you land about where QuickBooks already has you. That is precisely why I am recommending Edge instead. It is the one that actually changes your number.
  • On a big roof, the customer will see a real figure, and some will reach for a check. A 4% adjustment on a $9,000 job is $360, and that is not invisible. Here is why I still recommend it: if they pay the card, the job costs you $0 to process. If they decide to write a check or send a bank transfer instead, the job also costs you close to nothing. You win either way, which is not true today. Today the card version of that same job costs you about $270 out of your own pocket.
  • Debit can be carved out so anyone paying by debit gets the lower price. That matters on your smaller repair calls, where a debit card is realistic and the adjustment would be the most noticeable.
  • You can switch programs later without reapplying. Same account, same QuickBooks sync, same equipment. If your mix changes and the AllProPay Rate ever becomes the better answer for you, we move you and it costs nothing.

So you do what most contractors do and nudge them toward a check, and then you wait, after you have already paid for the material. On Edge that same job costs you $0 to run on a card, and the money is in your account the next morning. That is the trade I am actually offering you. It is less about the fee and more about not having to care how they pay.
