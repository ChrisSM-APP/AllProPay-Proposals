The Flexibility You Asked For

The reason this matters for your business specifically: you do not run cards every day, but when you do, the ticket can be five figures. One rate structure does not fit both situations. So the account is built to flex.

  • Use Path A when the customer is reasonable. You absorb a lower rate than Square and the customer sees nothing different at checkout.
  • Use Path B when the customer is the one demanding to pay by card. The terminal calculates the surcharge, prints it on the receipt with the dollar amount, and the customer chooses to proceed or hand you a check.
  • Switch any time. Same terminal. Same account. No paperwork to change the model. Run a few months on Path A, see the actual statement, and move to Path B if it makes more sense for your mix.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • Square's 3.5% keyed rate is the real bleeder. The wife-pay-by-card scenarios you described run keyed. That is where the math goes upside down on you fastest.
  • The $18k ticket has a clean answer. Surcharge program runs the card, the customer sees the fee on the receipt, and you deposit the full estimate amount the next morning.
  • PNC banking stays separate. You keep your relationship with them on the deposits side and avoid getting roped into a multi-year processing contract attached to that.
  • Auto body work on large insurance jobs gets disputed. Surcharge disclosure on the receipt plus a signed authorization on file tightens your defense if the customer ever calls the card company after the repair.
  • No contract on either side. If a month goes by and you hate it, you call and we shut it off. That is not a promise PNC will make.

Worked example on your open ticket: $18,000 keyed on Square at 3.5 percent costs you $630 in processing. Run that same charge on Path B, the customer covers the surcharge, you deposit $18,000. That single ticket pays the platform fee for the next twenty months.
