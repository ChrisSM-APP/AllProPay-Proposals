You are already close to $0 today, so I will not dress this up as savings. Here is what actually changes. A fee you add by hand is a surcharge, and surcharges come with card-network rules: the program has to be registered before the first sale, and the disclosure has to be in front of the customer before they pay. A line typed into a QuickBooks invoice does neither. On Edge the same 3.5% your customers pay today becomes a registered program, the wording sits on every invoice and on the terminal screen, and you never calculate or key anything again. There is a second thing worth checking on your own QuickBooks fee report: QuickBooks charges its keyed-in rate on the whole amount, your 3.5% included, so the 3.5% you collect is at best a wash. With me it is a clean $0. Then there is your time. If the fee math and the hand-keying take even an hour a week, that is about 50 hours a year. At a $125 shop hour, call it $6,500 a year of your time going to bookkeeping instead of cars.

Why This Is the Right Move
  • Your cost to accept a card is $0, and the fee is worked out and collected for you instead of by hand.
  • Your customers see the same 3.5% they see today. Nothing changes on their side except that it is printed and disclosed properly.
  • No more keying card numbers into QuickBooks. Tap or chip at the counter, a pay link on the invoice, and the payment posts back to QuickBooks Online.
  • A keyed-in card is the weakest kind of transaction in a dispute. A chip or tap at the counter shifts that liability, and if a dispute does come in you call me, not a call center.
  • No contract, no setup fee, no cancellation fee, nothing to buy. If we stop earning it, you walk.
