Where We Are

Fredarius, thanks for the time today. You told me you invoice out of QuickBooks, your customers pay online, a project runs $2,500 and up, and you would rather be paid in cash. That last part makes sense: at about 3%, a $2,500 job paid by card costs you around $75 before the money lands, and there is no way to lower that where you are. So this page is built so the card stops being the expensive answer. Cash, check, and bank transfer customers pay your price. A customer who wants to put the job on a card can, and the fee sits on their side of the invoice. QuickBooks does not move.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • Every card that pays a QuickBooks invoice costs you one flat rate, about 3%. On a $2,500 job that is about $75. On a $5,000 job, about $150. The bigger the project, the bigger the bite.
  • At about $10,000 a month in card payments that is roughly $300 a month, about $3,600 a year, coming off the top of finished work.
  • You prefer cash for a reason, and this keeps that reason on your side. The cash price on the invoice is your price. A customer who chooses the card pays the card price, so saying yes to a card no longer costs you anything.
  • QuickBooks is the part you want to keep. Understood. Nothing on this page moves your books, your invoices, or how your customers pay them.

Nothing new for you to learn between jobs.
Backed and flexible

Your QuickBooks, Upgraded

Your books do not move. Same QuickBooks, same customers, same invoices, same reports. You are not learning a new system and you are not rebuilding anything.

You get the tools QuickBooks does not give you. Send an invoice or a payment link by text, email, or QR code, keep a card on file for a repeat customer or a property manager, take a deposit on a job before the crew shows up, and take bank payments on the big invoices at the cash price. Card and bank deposits land separately, and each payment links back to its bundled daily deposit, so a lumped deposit matches in QuickBooks in one confirm instead of by hand.

The cash price runs right on your invoices. Intuit does not offer this at all, so it is the one piece you cannot get by staying where you are.

The Flexibility You Asked For

You run a painting business, not a payments desk, so the program bends to how you already work. You decide job by job, and nothing about it is locked.

  • Absorb the fee yourself on any job you want to look after. A repeat customer, a referral you want to keep sweet: one setting on that invoice and it runs at the cash price on a card. That call stays yours, per invoice.
  • Customers who would rather not pay the card price have a way to pay online anyway. Bank transfer on the same invoice, at the cash price. Fewer checks to wait on, fewer trips to the bank.
