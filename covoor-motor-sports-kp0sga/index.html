You are doing the right thing in principle. Passing the fee to the customer is the cleanest way to run a high-volume operation. The question is whether the program you have actually does what it claims, and whether the monthly cost is set right for your volume.
What we found

1. Square's surcharge does not fully cover your processing

Square's surcharge feature was added in 2023. It works, but it has real gaps:

  • Debit cards cannot be surcharged. Card-brand rules and California law both prohibit surcharging on debit. Every debit transaction you run, you pay Square's rate out of pocket. A typical motor sports merchant sees 20â€"35% of card volume on debit.
  • Square takes a percentage of the surcharge itself. You charge the customer 3%, Square processes that 3% as part of the total, and a slice of it stays with Square.
  • The 3% does not always match Square's rate. Square's terminal rate is 2.6% + $0.10. On premium and rewards cards the real cost can run higher than 3%, and you absorb the difference.

On $250K a month, even a small residual gap adds up. We estimate Square is still keeping $50 to $150 a month in net processing fees on top of your $200 service charge.

2. $200 a month is high for your volume

$200 a month for software and service is built around Square's bundle (POS subscription, online tools, add-ons). For a counter-based motor sports operation running on a terminal, you are paying for features you don't use. At $250K a month in volume, a clean dual pricing setup with terminal support should run closer to $59 to $99 a month all-in.

That is $100 to $140 a month back in your pocket on fixed cost alone.

3. California has specific rules for surcharging and dual pricing

California allows surcharging on credit cards (up to the merchant's actual cost, capped at 3%) and allows dual pricing (a "cash price" and a "credit price" posted at the point of sale). Both must be clearly disclosed to the customer before the sale. Square's setup is partially compliant. A purpose-built dual pricing program handles the disclosure, the receipt language, and the debit handling correctly.

If a card-brand audit or a state review looked at the way fees are currently being passed on a Square terminal, there is meaningful risk of being told to restructure. The setup we recommend removes that risk.
