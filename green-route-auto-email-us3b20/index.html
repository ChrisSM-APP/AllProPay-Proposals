Where We Are

Here is what we talked through, written down so you have it in one place. You are opening the doors and you are already running invoices and online payments out of QuickBooks. Nothing about that has to change. What changes is what each card costs you, and what you get for the money you are already spending.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • QuickBooks stays. You keep invoicing the way you do now, same books, same workflow, same customers clicking the same kind of pay link.
  • You are on a flat rate today, somewhere around 2.9% to 3.2% on every card. That one price never moves, no matter what card the customer hands you.
  • I am putting you on true-cost pricing instead: interchange, the real wholesale cost set by Visa and Mastercard, plus a flat 0.50% and 5 cents. That flat markup is the entire amount we make on a sale.
  • You get a countertop terminal, the online gateway for invoices and payment links, and tap-to-pay on your phone for when you are out at the car. One account fee covers all three, and it is in the table below.
  • No contract, no setup fee, no cancellation fee. If we stop earning it, you leave.
What You Get
QuickBooks does not move
We connect straight to QuickBooks. You invoice the way you already do, payments land where they already land, and your books stay yours. We have done this with thousands of merchants, so it is a setup step, not an experiment. You will not be rebuilding anything or learning a new system the week you open.
True-cost card pricing
You pay interchange, which is the real cost of the card set by Visa and Mastercard, plus a flat 0.50% and 5 cents a transaction. That is the whole markup. A flat rate charges you the same price on a basic debit card that costs almost nothing to run as it does on a premium rewards card that costs plenty. On true-cost pricing you stop subsidising the difference and start keeping it.
Ready to take money on day one
A countertop terminal for people paying at the lot, the online gateway behind your QuickBooks invoices and payment links, and tap-to-pay on your phone for a deposit taken standing next to the car. A named person to call, chargeback paperwork handled with you, and PCI compliance kept current as part of the service rather than billed as an extra.

Room to move later

You are a new business, so the honest answer is that your volume and your card mix will both look different in six months than they do in week one. This program is built to move with that. Nothing here is a lock-in, and there is a second page in your hands that takes your card cost to zero entirely whenever you decide you want it.

  • Start on true-cost pricing. Your customers see nothing different at all.
  • Move to Edge Plus on the same account whenever you want your card cost at zero. No new application, no new equipment, and you can see the whole thing priced out on the other page I sent you.
  • Go back and forth if you change your mind. It is one account either way.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee, so none of this is a door you cannot walk back through.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • Interchange plus a flat 0.50% and 5 cents. That is the entire markup over the wholesale cost of the card. No tiers, no padding, and nothing that quietly moves later.
  • You stop paying one flat price on every card. A flat rate overcharges you badly on debit and everyday cards. On true-cost pricing that difference stays with you instead.
  • QuickBooks stays exactly where it is. We connect to it. Your invoicing, your books, and your customers' experience do not change.
  • Terminal, online gateway, and tap-to-pay on your phone. One account fee covers all three, so you are set up for the lot, the invoice, and the deposit taken outside.
  • No contract, no setup fee, no cancellation fee. PCI compliance is part of the service, not a line on your bill.
  • Zero card cost is on the table whenever you want it. The Edge Plus page shows you exactly what that looks like, and it runs on this same account.

You told me you do not know your monthly card volume yet, which is fair enough when you are just opening, so here is the math on a $10,000 month and you can scale it in your head. At the flat 2.9% to 3.2% you are on today, $10,000 in card sales costs you roughly $290 to $320, and that is the same price whether the customer taps a basic debit card or a premium travel card. On interchange plus 0.50% and 5 cents, the processing on that same $10,000 lands somewhere around $220 to $280 depending on your card mix, and the account fee is $25, so call it $245 to $305 all in. On a month that size the gap is real but it is not life changing, and I would rather tell you that now than dress it up. Where this pulls away is volume and debit: every extra $10,000 you run widens it, because the $25 does not grow and the flat rate never comes down. That is also exactly why I put the Edge Plus page in front of you, and why I would rather you look at both than take my word for either.
