How Square pricing works: Square's flat rate of 2.6% + $0.10 applies to every card, every time -- debit and credit are treated the same. Regulated debit cards (most bank-issued debit cards) have interchange costs below 0.5% + $0.22. On a flat-rate plan, that difference goes to Square. With interchange-plus pricing, that lower cost gets passed through to you.
What We Found

Flat-rate pricing hides your actual cost

Square charges the same 2.6% whether a customer pays with a low-cost regulated debit card or a premium rewards credit card. The actual interchange on a debit card can be as low as 0.05% + $0.21. On a flat-rate plan, you absorb the difference on every debit transaction with no credit for the lower cost.

No relief as volume grows

Square's flat rate does not change with volume. At $2,000/month you pay ~$53. At $10,000/month you pay ~$265. At $25,000/month you pay ~$660. The rate never improves. With interchange-plus pricing, your effective rate often decreases as your debit-card mix settles and your negotiating position strengthens.

No itemized statement = no visibility

Square provides a summary, not an itemized statement. You cannot see how many debit vs. credit transactions you processed, what interchange category each transaction settled into, or where the fees are actually coming from. That makes it impossible to optimize your setup or benchmark your costs against alternatives.
