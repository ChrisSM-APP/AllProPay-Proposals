Your three options

You have flexibility in how processing fees are paid. We can set up Hairapy any of three ways, and you can switch between them later if you change your mind.

Option 1. Cash discount
Customers cover the fees

A small surcharge (typically around 4%) is added at checkout. The customer pays it. You receive 100% of the ticket.

Your effective cost: $0 in processing fees, plus the $25 monthly platform fee.
  • Customers see a line item on their receipt showing the cash discount or service fee.
  • Fully legal and Visa/Mastercard-compliant when the disclosure is done correctly. We handle that for you.
Option 2. Split
You and your customer share the fees

You decide the split. Examples: customer pays 3%, you absorb 1%. Or 2% / 2%. Any combination you want.

Useful for: Repeat clients who would push back on a full surcharge, where you still want some relief from current fees.
  • You absorb less than today, customers absorb less than a full cash-discount setup.
  • Same $25 monthly platform fee applies.
Option 3. Interchange-plus (IC+)
You absorb the fees, like Square

You pay the processing fees yourself, just like you do with Square today. The difference is the pricing structure. Square charges a flat rate (2.6% + $0.10 swiped, 2.9% + $0.30 keyed). That flat rate bakes in extra margin. Interchange-plus passes through the actual card-network cost (interchange) and adds a small, transparent markup on top.

Typical savings vs Square: 0.5 to 1.0 percentage points lower on your effective rate. On $6,000/month of card volume, roughly $30 to $60/month, or $360 to $720 a year.
  • Same tax treatment as Square - fees you pay are still a deductible business expense.
  • Same $25 monthly platform fee applies.
Your tax-deduction question, answered honestly

You said your CPA mentioned you take processing fees as a deduction at year-end, and you wanted to make sure changing setups would not cost you that.

The short answer: your deduction does not disappear, but it changes depending on which option you pick.

Setup What you pay in fees What you deduct
Square today (you absorb) ~2.6% + $0.10 per swipe The full fee amount, every year
Option 1 - Cash discount (customer pays) $0 Nothing - because you did not pay any fees
Option 2 - Split Your portion only Your portion
Option 3 - IC+ (you absorb) ~1.5 to 2.0% effective The full fee amount (smaller than today)
The math that matters. A deduction reduces what you pay in taxes by a fraction of the expense, not the whole thing. If you are in a 24% tax bracket and you pay $100 in fees, you get $24 back at tax time. You are still out $76 net. If instead you pay $0 in fees (cash discount), there is no $24 refund, but you also did not pay the $76. You are $76 ahead. The deduction question is real - just make sure your CPA is comparing total dollars kept, not total dollars deducted.
