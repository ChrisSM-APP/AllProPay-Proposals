What We Found

1. You are absorbing 100% of the processing cost

This is a business model choice, not a fee structure issue. Plenty of auto repair shops in your range pass processing costs to card-paying customers through a small surcharge or a cash-discount program. We are not recommending you change that - most shops have strong reasons to keep customer pricing simple. We are flagging it because the option exists if you ever want it, and the math on a fee-pass program would eliminate the processing cost almost entirely.

2. A 3% effective rate points to bundled or tiered pricing

Processors often run on bundled pricing where a single headline rate covers all card types - credit, debit, rewards, business cards, the whole mix. That model is simple, but it overcharges on debit-heavy volume, which auto repair shops typically have. Interchange-plus passes the actual wholesale cost of each card type through to you, then adds a small fixed markup. Debit cards cost about 0.30% at wholesale. On a tiered plan you might be paying 2.5% or higher on the same card. That gap is where most of the savings live.

3. Without a statement, this is the most honest version of the math

Either way, you find out what you are actually paying - and that is worth the five minutes whether you switch or not.
