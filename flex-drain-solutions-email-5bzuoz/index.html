Where We Are

You let us know two things on the call that matter more than any rate. Most of your customers pay by check, and your card volume dropped a lot once you started adding the processing fee to the invoice. Those are the same problem looked at from two sides, and I want to fix both. Make the fee on the invoice smaller, so fewer people reach for a checkbook. And give the ones who still prefer their bank account a way to pay you online for ten dollars or less. You are setting Housecall Pro up now, so this is the right week to do it.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • Housecall Pro stays exactly as you are setting it up. Your jobs, your invoices, and your customer history do not move. We connect the payments underneath it.
  • You keep charging the processing fee if you want to. Nothing about the way you bill has to change.
  • The fee your customer sees gets smaller, because what the card actually costs you gets smaller.
  • Your check writers get a bank payment option, capped at ten dollars a job however big the job is, so you stop waiting on paper.
What You Get
Housecall Pro stays. Payments plug in underneath.
You are mid-setup, which is the cheapest time to get this right. Invoices keep going out of Housecall Pro and your customers keep paying from the link in them. What changes is the pricing behind the link: the real cost of each card plus one flat markup you can see, instead of a single bundled rate set inside somebody else's software.
True-cost pricing, and you can check it
You pay interchange, the real cost of the card set by Visa and Mastercard, plus a flat 0.50 percent and 10 cents a transaction. That is the entire markup and it does not move month to month. One monthly service fee covers the Housecall Pro connection, the payment links, statements, PCI, and support. No setup fee, no annual fee, no contract, and no cancellation fee. You also get a named service rep who answers the phone and handles chargeback paperwork with you.
A real answer for the people who write checks
A bank payment does what a check does, without the paper. It runs at 0.75 percent and it is capped at ten dollars no matter the size of the job. On a $1,500 drain line that is ten dollars against roughly thirty-eight on a card, and the money lands in your account instead of in somebody's glove box for a week. It sits on the same invoice as the card option, so the customer picks.
