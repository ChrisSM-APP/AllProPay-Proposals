What This Means

1. You are paying counter-top rates for work that is never at a counter

Your jobs are booked and paid online or keyed after the visit, so every card you take downgrades to the most expensive tier Fiserv has. 73% of your Visa volume in December ran at 3.15% plus 10 cents under a label that says non-qualified. That is not a bad card mix; it is a pricing model that punishes a mobile business for being mobile.

2. Your processor keeps more than the card networks do

Of the $1,853.87 you paid in December, about $840 was the true cost of the cards and about $1,014 was Fiserv. $620.04 of that arrived as a single month-end charge with no line items behind it. Option B takes the processor share down to about $436; Option A takes the whole line to $0.

3. Last time the difference was small. This time it is the whole number.

A rate cut can only ever shave what you pay, and on a card-not-present book with 21% Amex there is a ceiling on how far that goes. Edge does not shave it. The card cost moves to the card-paying customer, disclosed before they pay, and your December fee line becomes $25.
