What This Means
1Growing made your rate worse, and it should not have
Volume up 67 percent, fees up 76 percent, rate up from 3.20 to 3.36 percent. On tiered pricing your rate drifts with your card mix and you cannot control it. On interchange-plus you pay the true cost of the card plus one flat markup, so a bigger month is just a bigger month. Worth an estimated $250 to $550 a month at July's volume.
2About half your bill is American Express's own cost, and I cannot touch it
Of the $4,755.68 you paid in July, roughly $2,400 is American Express's program cost. That is a pass-through, the same with me or anyone else, and I would rather say so now than have you find it on the comparison. It is the honest reason my number is $3,000 to $6,600 a year and not something larger. What I can take off is the $807 in downgrade penalties and part of the markup on top.
3Your biggest lever is not the rate, it is how the big invoices get paid
Your average American Express ticket in July was $4,062, and each one costs you roughly $130 in fees. Bank transfer on your account runs 0.50 percent and is capped. If even one in three of those large invoices settled that way, that is roughly $800 a month on its own, more than the rate change is worth. Nothing is forced, and customers who want their points keep paying by card. It is simply an option you do not have today.
