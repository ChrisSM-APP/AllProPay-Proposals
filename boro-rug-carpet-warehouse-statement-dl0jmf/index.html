What This Means
1Growing made your rate worse, and it should not have
Volume up 67 percent, fees up 76 percent, rate up from 3.20 to 3.36 percent. On tiered pricing your rate drifts with your card mix and you cannot control it. On interchange-plus you pay the true cost of the card plus one flat markup, so a bigger month is just a bigger month.
2Your Visa and Mastercard ran 3.48 percent, and $807 of it was penalty
Your Visa and Mastercard came to $44,152 in July and cost $1,537, which is 3.48 percent. Of that, $807 was downgrade penalty, not a rate you agreed to. On interchange-plus that bucket does not exist, and keying phone orders with address verification and an order number attached is what stops a business card downgrading in the first place. Worth an estimated $300 to $480 a month.
3Your biggest lever is not the rate, it is how the big invoices get paid
Your average American Express ticket in July was $4,062, and each one costs you roughly $130. Bank transfer runs 0.50 percent, capped. If one in three of those large invoices settled that way, that is roughly $800 a month, more than the rate change is worth. Nothing is forced, and customers who want their points keep using their card. The proposal also sets out a further option where the posted price becomes the card price and cash, check or bank transfer gets a discount: that takes your card cost to zero, roughly $45,000 a year at your two-month average.
