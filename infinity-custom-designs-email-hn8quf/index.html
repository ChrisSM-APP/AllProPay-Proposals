Each card priced at what that card costs
Square bills one flat price on everything, 2.6 percent and 15 cents, whether the customer hands you a plain debit card or a premium rewards card. Built for the $2,000 jobs

The terminal arrives programmed and ready to plug in, with a virtual terminal on your computer or phone for anything you take over the phone.

The Option You Were Told About

You pay the card fee yourself today and you want to keep doing that, so that is how this account is built. Kurt mentioned the other way on your first call, though, so here it is in one paragraph in case you ever want it. Nothing on this page depends on you choosing it.

  • You start here, absorbing the fee. Exactly as you do now, at the rates in the table above. No change at your counter.
  • If you ever want to stop paying it, the same account can post a cash price and a card price. The customer paying by card covers the fee and your card cost goes to zero. No new application and no new terminal.
  • You can do it on some jobs and not others. Plenty of shops pass it on the big installs and absorb it on the small stuff. That switch is yours, not ours.
  • Either way there is no contract. Month to month. If we stop earning it, you leave and you owe nothing.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • You get a person by name, not an inbox. My direct line and my cell for a text, with 24/7 phone support on the processing side behind me. When something needs sorting out you are not filling in a form and waiting.
  • Each card is priced at what that card costs. Interchange plus a flat 0.40 percent and 5 cents a transaction, which is the whole markup. A plain debit sale stops paying the same price as a premium rewards card.
  • A $2,000 job paid by bank transfer costs $10, not $52. Bank transfers are 0.75 percent capped at $10 per payment, so the fee stops growing with the size of the job. On tickets your size this is the biggest line on the page.

  • No contract, no setup fee, no PCI fee, no statement fee, no cancellation fee. One monthly fee covers the account, the terminal, statements, PCI and support. If we stop earning it you walk.

Here is the arithmetic on one $2,000 job, which is the kind of sale this is really about. On Square you pay 2.6 percent plus 15 cents, about $52, and it is $52 whether the customer used a plain debit card or a premium rewards card. Under true-cost pricing that same job costs roughly $48 to $54 on a rewards credit card, which on the very richest cards is close to what you pay now, and as low as about $12 on a plain debit card, because debit genuinely costs a fraction of credit. If they pay it by bank transfer, it is $10. That is the whole idea: you stop paying one price for every card and start paying what each one actually costs. These are estimates based on what you shared on the call, not a guarantee. One month of your Square deposits makes them exact.
