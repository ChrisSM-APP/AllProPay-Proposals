The Flexibility You Asked For

You said you do not have time for a project, so here is the honest version of what changes and what does not. There are two ways to run this, the pricing above is the same either way, and you do not have to decide today.

  • Beside Tekmetric, which is what I recommend. Cards at the counter go through a terminal. Invoices go out of Tekmetric exactly as they do now, with our payment link alongside, and the customer pays on the link. You mark the repair order paid. It is one extra step per payment, and at the handful of payments a day a shop your size takes, it is nothing. This is the fastest path and it changes nothing about how you work.
  • Connected inside Tekmetric, if you want it. There is an extension that puts the payment inside the Tekmetric screen so there is no second step at all. It is a bigger setup and it carries its own software cost, so I will not quote you a number for it until I have confirmed it for your exact setup. Worth a conversation only if the extra step genuinely bothers you.
  • Bank transfer on the large bills. 0.75 percent, capped at $10 a transaction, on the same link. Your call per job.
  • And if you ever want your card cost at zero. Some shops price cash and card differently and post it at the counter, which takes the card cost off the business entirely. You absorb it today and nothing here asks you to change that. The same account does it whenever you say so, with no new application.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • The markup is in writing and it is 0.25 percent plus 5 cents. Every brand, no tiers, no downgrade categories, no padding. You can check it against any statement you ever get from us.
  • At $200,000 a month, every 0.10 percent of markup is about $200. That is the whole reason this is worth twenty minutes of your time, and it is arithmetic on your own number rather than a claim about anyone's rate.
  • One $25 monthly fee, and that is the only fixed fee there is. No PCI fee, no statement fee, no batch fee, no annual fee. We keep you PCI compliant as part of the service.

Here is the arithmetic, on the $200,000 a month you gave me. Interchange is a pass-through cost and it is the same wherever you process, so it cancels out of any comparison. What does not cancel out is the markup on top of it. At $200,000 a month, a tenth of a percent of markup is about $200 a month, which is roughly $2,400 a year. So if Provident is keeping half a percent and I am keeping a quarter, that is about $500 a month, call it $6,000 a year, and if they are keeping less than that the gap is smaller. I genuinely do not know which it is yet, and that is exactly why I want one statement instead of guessing in front of you. These are estimates built on the volume you described, not a guarantee, and one statement turns all of them into exact figures.
