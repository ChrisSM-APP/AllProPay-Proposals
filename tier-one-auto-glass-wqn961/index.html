Where We Are

You asked me two things before you would take any of this at face value. Prove the Omega side actually works, and get you a real answer on buy now pay later instead of a guess. I have both now, in writing, and one of them is not the answer I was hoping for. You get it either way. Your shop runs on Omega EDI and it is not moving. What changes is how the money lands and who carries the card fee on the work your customers pay for directly.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • Omega EDI does not move. Parts, VIN lookups, insurance billing, and job status all stay exactly where they are. Your counter still sees paid or not paid in real time before a vehicle leaves the bay.
  • Buy now pay later is an Omega limit, not a processor limit. I put it to the processor in writing and got it back the same way. Omega EDI does not support buy now pay later. The gateway side can carry it, but Omega has to be able to hand it off and it cannot, so no gateway change makes it show up inside Omega. Moving to us does not add it and does not take it away. If you want to keep offering it, you keep the tool you use for it now and run it alongside. The only way to get it inside Omega is a different shop platform, and I am not going to tell you to replace Omega over one payment option.
  • Your counter and van terminals are standalone units. They do not route through Omega, which is what makes the cash-price and card-price structure possible on that work with no change to your software at all.
  • Your invoiced and insurance work is priced differently, and honestly. You cannot put a card fee on an insurance carrier, so that side runs on true-cost pricing instead. I am not going to tell you your whole book goes to zero.
  • You get paid gross on both accounts. On the counter and van work the deposit is the full amount of the transaction, because the fee is already handled at the register. On the Omega and insurance side we set the account to monthly discounting, so the fees come out once a month as one line instead of being carved out of every batch.
  • Debit is carved out. The adjustment applies to credit cards only. Cash, check, and debit customers all pay the lower posted price, which was your concern with the rules here.
What You Get
Zero-cost card processing
On the counter and the vans, the card cost moves off the business and onto the credit-card-paying customer. Cash, check, and debit customers pay the lower posted price. That is the volume where a zero card cost is genuinely achievable, and it is the volume you have the most control over.
Two lanes, priced for what each one actually is
Counter and field work that your customer pays for directly runs on the cash-price and card-price structure, so the card cost on that volume comes off your books. Work you invoice through Omega and bill to carriers runs on interchange plus a flat 0.55 percent and 5 cents a transaction, because a carrier cannot be charged a card fee. Two accounts, one setup, and you never think about which is which. The terminal and the software each just do their job.
Backed and flexible
Direct underwriting, next-day funding, no contract and no cancellation fee. If you ever want to absorb the fee yourself instead, we move you to flat interchange-plus on the same account.
