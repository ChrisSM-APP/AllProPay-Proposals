Where We Are

Here is what you told me on the phone, so you know I was listening. You pay about 3 percent, you pass that 3 percent on to your customers, and you are getting pushback on it. You do not want them paying more. Fair enough. So the plan is not to change what you charge them. The plan is to bring down what the card actually costs you, which is the only honest way that 3 percent ever comes down.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • Keep taking payments exactly how you do now: in person on the lot, and by invoice for the deals that get done over the phone or by email.
  • Move your card cost to true cost. You pay interchange, the real price Visa and Mastercard set, plus one small flat markup. Nothing hidden on top of it.
  • Keep passing the fee on if you want to. That is still your call. The difference is you would be passing on a smaller number instead of a flat 3 percent.
  • Take big invoices by bank transfer for a flat 45 cents up to a $10 maximum, so a large down payment stops costing a percentage at all.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee. If we ever stop earning it, you walk.
What You Get
Nothing on the lot changes
You keep running deals the way you run them today, in person and by invoice, and you keep your own software for VIN work and estimates. We are changing what the card costs you, not how you sell cars. I handle the paperwork and the setup with you.
True-cost pricing
You pay interchange, the real cost of the card set by Visa and Mastercard, plus a flat 0.45 percent and 10 cents a transaction. That is the entire markup. There are no tiers, no padding, and no surprise line items. On a setup like yours that lands somewhere around 2.1 to 2.6 percent all-in, against the 3 percent you are running today.
Bank transfer on the big ones
For a large down payment or a deal invoiced out, bank transfer costs 0.75 percent capped at $10. On a $3,000 payment that is $10 instead of $90. For a car lot that alone is worth the conversation, and your customer pays nothing extra.

Who pays the fee stays your call

You already pass the fee on, so you know exactly how this works. Nothing about that has to change. What changes is the size of the number you are passing on. Right now you are asking a customer to cover 3 percent, and they are pushing back on it, because 3 percent on a car is real money. Bring your own cost down to true cost and you have room to move: charge them less, charge them nothing, or keep it where it is and keep the difference yourself. One account, and you can change your mind later without new paperwork or new equipment.
