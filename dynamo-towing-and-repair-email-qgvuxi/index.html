You never gave me a monthly card number, and I am not going to invent one to make this page look good. So here is what moves per payment instead. On a card invoice today QuickBooks takes 2.6% to 3.5% out of what you billed. On Edge your cost on that sale is $0, because the processing sits with the customer who chose to put it on a card, and anyone paying by bank transfer, check or cash pays the plain invoice total. On bank transfers QuickBooks charges 1% capped at $20. With me it is 0.75% capped at $10, so on your bigger tow and repair invoices that cap is the half you stop paying. That lane is where most of your money already comes in.

Your Two Ways To Do This

You asked me to lower or get rid of the card cost. Those are two different answers and you should see both priced, not just the one I like.

0.50% + $0.10
my flat markup, on top of the card networks' own cost
  • Interchange is the wholesale cost Visa and Mastercard set on each card. Every processor pays it, QuickBooks included, and it is already inside their 2.6% to 3.5%.
  • The difference is what the processor keeps on top. Mine is 0.50% and a dime, in writing, and it does not change by card type.
  • Debit and plain consumer cards are where this one pays off hardest, because you stop paying a credit-card rate on them.
  • Nothing changes for your customers. They see the invoice total and that is it.

Option B lowers what you pay. Option A removes it. The setup, the QuickBooks sync and the monthly cost are the same either way, and you can move between them on the same account later without filling anything out again.
