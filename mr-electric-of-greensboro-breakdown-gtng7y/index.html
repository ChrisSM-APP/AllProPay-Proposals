What This Means

1. Your rate is already reasonable, and I can still lower it

Propeller keeps about 0.28% over the true cost of the card, which is genuinely tight. I can take that to about 0.19% all in and clear five fees off your statement entirely, and Option B does that. But be clear about the size of it: a third off a small number is still a small number. If a competitor tells you they can cut your rate dramatically, ask them what their markup is and compare it to 0.28%.

2. Your customers pay with the most expensive cards there are

Corporate cards, purchasing cards, and business rewards cards ran a large share of your August volume, and those carry the highest interchange the networks publish, up to 3.15%. Before anyone adds a markup, your card cost was already 1.96%. No processor can negotiate that down, because no processor sets it.

3. The big lever is who pays the fee, not what the fee is

Lowering your rate is worth about $38 a month. Moving who covers the cost is worth about $1,082 a month, because it takes the whole $1,097 line off your books rather than shaving the small part of it your processor keeps. Both are real offers and I will write either one. That is just the difference in size, and you should see it before you choose.
