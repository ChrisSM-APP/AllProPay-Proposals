What We Typically Find

1. Tiered pricing -- the most common and costly setup in auto repair

The majority of auto repair shops in California are on a tiered or bundled pricing model, usually set up through their bank or a local ISO rep. On a tiered model, transactions are sorted into "qualified," "mid-qualified," and "non-qualified" buckets. The merchant rarely knows which transactions land where, and non-qualified rates can run 3.5% or higher.

Interchange-plus pricing eliminates the sorting game entirely. You see the actual card-brand cost plus a fixed, disclosed markup. For businesses with a significant mix of rewards or business credit cards -- which is most auto repair shops -- this typically produces a meaningfully lower effective rate.

2. Monthly fees that never get reviewed

PCI non-compliance fees, statement fees, regulatory fees, and batch fees accumulate quietly on every statement. Most merchants have never had someone walk them through what each line item actually means or whether it belongs there. A typical auto repair statement has $40 to $90 in fees that are either unnecessary or priced well above market rate.

3. High-ticket transactions -- high exposure to rewards card interchange

Transmission repairs and auto service jobs carry average tickets well above most retail businesses. That means more exposure to corporate cards, travel rewards cards, and premium consumer credit products -- all of which carry higher interchange. If the current pricing model does not account for this card mix, Nor-Cal Transmissions is absorbing that premium on every transaction.
