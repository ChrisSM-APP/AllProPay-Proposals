Where We Are

When we spoke you told me the processing cost had gotten out of hand, and that you would rather the card fee sat with the customer paying by card than come out of the job. You take payments at the counter and keyed in over the phone, the volume swings a lot month to month, and some invoices are big enough that you want a cheaper way to collect them. That is exactly what this is built around.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • The card fee moves off the business and onto the customer who chooses to pay by card.
  • Both ways you take payment are covered at the same rate: at the counter and keyed in over the phone.
  • A cheaper lane for the large invoices, so a big job does not carry a card fee at all.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee, because you are in the middle of comparing and you should be able to leave anyone who stops earning it.
What You Get
Zero-cost card processing
On the Edge program the card fee moves to the card-paying customer as a small, clearly labeled adjustment. Cash, check, and ACH customers pay your listed price. On a card sale, your business keeps effectively all of it.
Same counter, same phone orders
Nothing about how you take a payment changes. The terminal sits where the current one sits and works the same way, and keyed-in phone orders run at the same 4% as a card handed across the counter. The only difference your team sees is the price display.
Backed and flexible
Direct underwriting, next-day funding, no contract and no cancellation fee. If you ever want to absorb the fee yourself instead, we move you to flat interchange-plus on the same account.

Right now every card sale costs the business money. A setup like yours usually lands around 3% once everything is added up, which is about $300 out of the business for every $10,000 you run on cards. On a $2,000 glass invoice that is roughly $60 gone before you have paid for the glass.

On Edge your cost on card sales is $0. The posted price is the card price, cash and check customers pay less, and the card-paying customer covers the 4% adjustment, disclosed before they pay. The $2,000 invoice deposits as $2,000. These are estimates based on what you shared on the call, not a statement, so one recent statement would let me replace the range with your exact number.

The Flexibility You Asked For

You are comparing two or three other people right now, so the account is built to stay flexible rather than lock you in.

  • Start on Edge with $0 card cost to the business. If you ever decide you would rather absorb the fee yourself, we move you onto flat interchange-plus pricing on the same account with no reapplication.
  • Use the ACH lane only when it helps. Large invoices go that way, everyday counter sales stay on the card rails.
  • Nothing is locked. No term, no cancellation fee. If we stop earning the account, you leave.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • Your cost on card sales goes to $0, in person and keyed in. Estimated $3,600 to $14,400 a year stays in the business, based on the months you described.
  • The volume swing stops mattering. A heavy month no longer costs you more, because the card cost is not yours.
  • Large invoices have a cheaper home. ACH caps at $10 no matter how big the job is.
  • New terminal included, programmed and ready. No lease and no equipment charge.
  • No contract, no setup fee, no cancellation fee. You can compare us against anyone without taking a risk to do it.

Take the busy months you mentioned. At about $40,000 on cards, a typical setup costs roughly $1,200 that month. On Edge that same month costs you nothing on card sales, and the slow months cost you nothing either. That is the part that does not move around with your volume.
