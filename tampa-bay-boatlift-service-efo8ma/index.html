Where We Are

Last time around I brought you a program that moved the card fee to your customers, and you were right to kill it. The moment the fee showed up at checkout your customers reached for a checkbook, and you went from same-week money to waiting 14 or 15 days for the mail. This is a different build. You absorb the fee, exactly as you do now, and the work goes into making the fee smaller and visible instead of flat and hidden.

You told us what mattered most. We built the setup around it:

  • You keep absorbing the processing cost. Nobody asks your customer to pay a fee, so nobody reaches for a checkbook and nothing slows your funding down.
  • QuickBooks does not move. Same invoices, same customers, same books, same workflow. The payments side improves underneath it.
  • About 90 percent of your card volume is American Express. Whatever your AMEX cost turns out to be, you see it, in writing, before you sign.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee. You already paid $1,500 to get out of one agreement. You are not signing another one.
What You Get
QuickBooks stays exactly where it is
You keep invoicing out of QuickBooks the way you do today. What changes is underneath: you can get paid by text, email, QR code, or a link in the invoice, take a card on file for repeat lift service, set up recurring invoices, run a card by hand, and take ACH. Your books and your workflow do not move an inch.
You see the card's cost, and you see what I keep
Visa, Mastercard, Discover, and American Express all publish what they charge. You pay that, at cost, and then a flat 0.35 percent and 5 cents a transaction on top. That is the entire markup and it never moves with the card. There is no tier, no qualified or non-qualified sorting, and no padding sitting inside a single flat number where you cannot see it.
Nothing holds you here but the pricing
Month to month from the first day. No contract, no term, no early termination fee, no equipment lease. If the numbers ever stop making sense you give notice and leave, and it costs you nothing. That is the only honest answer to what your last processor did to you.

Your QuickBooks, Upgraded

The payments layer we put under QuickBooks does something Intuit does not. Your deposits already land in your bank as one lump covering a batch of invoices. This links every single payment back to the deposit it was bundled into, so QuickBooks finds the match itself instead of you tying it out by hand. Card deposits and ACH deposits come through separately, so your reporting stays clean.

  • You keep QuickBooks Online. Nothing about how you bill changes. The integration sits beside your books, it does not replace them.
  • Better tools than QuickBooks Payments gives you. Pay by text, email, QR code, or link. Card on file. Recurring and scheduled invoices. Virtual terminal. ACH.
  • Your lumped deposits reconcile themselves. Each payment carries its deposit date and batch, so a single deposit covering two dozen invoices matches in one confirm.
Why This Is the Right Move
  • The markup is 0.35 percent and 5 cents a transaction, on every card type. AMEX included. There is no separate AMEX rate for me to pad, because I do not set the AMEX rate. You pay what American Express charges, plus the same 0.35 percent you pay on a Visa.
  • On Visa, Mastercard, and Discover the gap is already clear. Published interchange on those cards runs well under 2.90 percent, so that part of your book comes down the day you switch. It is a smaller slice of your volume than AMEX, but it is the part nobody has to confirm.
  • The checks are not free either. QuickBooks takes 1 percent of every bank transfer, capped at $20. Bank payments on your account run 0.75 percent, capped at $10. Every invoice a customer pays from their bank costs you less.
  • One monthly fee of $50, all in. That covers the QuickBooks integration, invoicing, card on file, the virtual terminal, statements, PCI, and support. No setup fee, no gateway fee, no PCI charge, no annual fee, no statement fee.
  • No contract and no cancellation fee. You paid $1,500 to leave the last one. You will not pay a dollar to leave this one.

Let me be straight with you about where the math stands, because you have been told a number before and had it turn out to be something else. At roughly $100,000 a month, QuickBooks keeps 2.90 percent of everything, about $2,900, no matter what the card underneath actually cost. On your account what I keep is 0.35 percent plus 5 cents a transaction, about $350 plus the transaction count. On the Visa and Mastercard side that is already a win, because published interchange on those cards sits well below 2.90 percent. I am not going to guess at it and hand you a savings figure built on a guess.
