What You Get
Your debit cards stop paying a credit card price
This is where the money is, and it is worth being specific about why. A flat rate is an average. It bills one price no matter which card the customer hands your tech, which means every plain debit tap in your book is quietly paying for somebody else's rewards card. We bill interchange, which is the real cost Visa and Mastercard set for that individual card, plus a flat 0.50 percent and 10 cents. On a $125 job, a plain debit card tapped in the field lands near 1 percent instead of the 2.5 percent you pay today on that same tap. Nothing about the job changes. The card just stops being billed as something it is not.
Tap on phone for all four teams, at no cost
Your techs take cards the same way tomorrow as they do today: tap on the phone, in the driveway, job done. The difference is that the tap costs you nothing to set up and nothing per tap beyond the rate in the table below. There is no reader to buy for four teams, no hardware lease and no per-device charge. Payment links come with the account too, so when somebody is on the fence about a repair you can still text them a link for the deposit and hold the job, exactly the way you do now.
Housecall Pro stays, and so does the person you call
You told me Housecall Pro is not really about invoicing for you, it is where the jobs, the scheduling, the photos and the history live, so that a customer who calls back can be found by address or phone number. None of that moves and none of it is at risk here. On the money side, you get a person instead of an inbox. If something looks wrong on a deposit you call or text me. If a customer ever disputes a charge, a card tapped in the field is a card-present sale, which is the strongest kind of record to have, and we put the response together with you rather than handing you a form. A chargeback costs $15 if one ever happens.
